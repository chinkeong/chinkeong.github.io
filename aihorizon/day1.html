01 · 10:05

Building an AI Ecosystem: from experimentation to industry

  • Raja Segaran · Director, Regional Digital Economy Office, MDEC

Malaysia's AI push is about balancing supply and demand, not just buying GPUs.

23.4%digital economy share of GDP (DOSM 2024)
100+AI solution providers in MDEC's network
  • Grow the cake, not just the slice. The digital share of GDP matters less than the size of the whole economy.
  • Five building blocks: local tech supply with its own IP (not only MNCs), real demand from industry, talent at every level from board to entry, compute, and data governance.
  • The bubble question: dot-com era fibre lasted 20–30 years. GPU spending is bigger and ages faster, so real demand has to keep pace.
  • Decide the trust level per process: human-in-the-loop, on-the-loop or out-of-the-loop, especially in regulated sectors.

MDEC's northern office in George Town matches companies with AI vendors and runs use-case labs. It's a free door to knock on.

02 · 10:30

ASUS Blackwell implementation for local LLM & OpenClaw

  • Dr. Ahmad Nizar Harun · CEO, MIMOS Academy

Run a small LLM locally for private data, and treat AI as a worker, not a chatbot.

6,000+people trained by MIMOS Academy
2 weekshow often the AI landscape shifts, in his words
  • Three levels of use: companion (think with it) → assistant (one draft flows through Excel, PowerPoint and more) → workers (a team of agents with skills such as finance, legal or accounting).
  • A local box: an ASUS Blackwell desktop runs small open models on-premises, so sensitive data never leaves the building. GPU memory shortages are pushing prices up.
  • Paper → knowledge base: scan old records to PDF and load them into a local LLM. One example correlated police investigation files to link suspects and victims. Another turned 10 years of paper maintenance logs into fast troubleshooting.
  • An always-on agent gives him a morning brief of every AI announcement.
  • Non-developers in his classes now build their own HR, point-of-sale and tutoring apps.

A local LLM over our own archive of reports is a low-risk first project.

03 · 10:55

Beyond the Grayscale: clarity for medical diagnostics & health screening

  • Justin Koh · Founder & Managing Director, VPara

AI ultrasound at GP-clinic prices could move screening out of hospitals.

<10 sto flag a lesion in the live demo
< gaming PCdevice cost, within a GP's budget
  • Live demo: a handheld ultrasound probe on a Samsung tablet scanned a breast phantom. The AI flagged a possible lesion and rated it BI-RADS 3 in under 10 seconds.
  • Leapfrogging: give screening tools to GP clinics instead of hospitals. Patients get earlier detection and clinics get a new source of revenue.
  • The doctor stays in charge. The tool raises a GP's capability. It doesn't replace the specialist.

An expensive specialist step becomes cheap triage, and the expert only sees escalations. The pattern travels well beyond healthcare.

04 · 11:20

Building a start-up with AI as infrastructure

  • Dato' Ts. Dr. Shanmuganathan Palanisamy · CEO, ECTrons

Build AI into the company from day one, with humans as the master and AI as the servant.

3 mo → 2 wkcomplex PCB design time with AI routing and layout
~1 monthlayout and software done in parallel
  • AI-native from day one: a “virtual workforce” of AI agents covers roles a small firm can't staff.
  • Watch the cost trap: swapping salaries for data-centre bills may just move the cost around.
  • Some jobs stay human-only. That rule is set at the foundation, not added later.
  • Virtual PCB: firmware is brought up on a digital board while the layout is still being done, so software is ready when the hardware lands.
  • “Innovation is not an exhibition, it's an assessment.” A small local company can compete with the big players.

Run hardware and software development in parallel on a digital twin instead of in sequence.

05 · 11:45

Gov AI Tani: AI field guidance for paddy farmers via WhatsApp

  • Bjarte Olsen · Managing Director, Global AI Village

Not a chatbot. A state machine per farmer, delivered on WhatsApp.

~50%Malaysia's rice self-sufficiency
<1,000farmers on the scheme today, against a 10,000 target
  • Meet users where they are: it runs on WhatsApp, so there's no app to download. Farmers use voice, text or photos, one-handed.
  • State, not Q&A: each farmer's ~120-day rice cycle is tracked stage by stage, from soil prep to sowing to harvest. When a step slips, the AI re-plans the rest.
  • Layers: research papers and expert know-how, plus satellite soil moisture and hyper-local weather.
  • Per-farmer data shows who is struggling, so the few field officers go where they're needed instead of visiting at random.
  • Mixed models: commercial and open-source models, with a router that picks the right one per task.
  • Next: use satellite data on paddy methane to nudge water practices, which could earn carbon credits. Subsidies and micro-insurance are planned too.

Any SOP-driven workforce (ships, construction, maintenance) can be modelled as states, with AI re-planning when reality drifts.

06 · 12:10

From crisis response to accredited excellence: sustainable digital health

  • Viva Shaik Farid · Director & General Manager, United Crest Healthcare

Grants built the system during COVID. Only a business model keeps it alive.

20×more Singapore doctors used telemedicine during COVID
2,500doctors certified in telemedicine
  • Continuous beats episodic: home monitoring with IoT devices instead of seeing patients only at the clinic. GPs can earn from it.
  • AI voice triage (during COVID): automated calls sorted enrolled patients into those who needed a doctor now and those who could wait.
  • Remote eye screening: fundus images are read at GP level and sent to an ophthalmologist only when needed.
  • AI is a force multiplier for clinicians, not a replacement for the care relationship.

Plan for when the funding stops: what pays for this once the pilot money is gone?

07 · 1:45

Panel: The AI Reality Check: challenges, lessons and breakthroughs

  • Moderator: Aziz Kevin Lee · MDEC RDEO
  • Calum Halcrow · HiveKind
  • Jan Henrik Bartscht · Leadapreneur
  • Chan Kee Siak · Exabytes
  • Kai Chin Low · CERTIS

The bottleneck isn't the model. It's data, approvals and people.

~400alerts a day at Jewel Changi, triaged by AI first
~1,000CCTV cameras watched by computer vision
~1,000roles Certis can never fill
  • Certis (25,000 staff): a 24/7 voice AI helpdesk serves frontline staff and gives faster fixes with a fraction of the people. AI checks CCTV before a human decides (“real fire or faulty sensor?”). About 100 patrol robots work at Singapore sites.
  • Leaks come from inside: one model was built on a customer's data and was caught in an audit. A staff member's personal AI use exposed private information.
  • Exabytes: common mistakes are too many tools, people experimenting alone with no company policy, and data left on outside platforms.
  • The approval bottleneck: 100 AI ideas and 5 approvers stalls everything, and good people leave.
  • “Slop grenades”: AI sounds confident, so beginners trust it and pass along work they don't understand.
  • The cheapest start: give operations teams AI for everyday tasks. No special use case is needed.
  • The human's job moves from doing to verifying, so set up checks and metrics for AI output. Physical-world work still needs human oversight.

Publish an approved-tools list and a simple data policy before usage spreads on its own.

08 · 2:50

Humanoid robots are coming. Is your business ready?

  • Edwin Yap · CEO, Mr. Robot

One wheeled humanoid instead of five separate automation cells.

6 taskspick, sort, inspect, pack, move and palletise, on one robot
10M+invested in a robot-training data centre
  • Wheels, not legs, for industry: faster and more stable. The upper body is human-shaped because human training video is abundant.
  • Replaces a multi-vendor stack: picking cell, sorting cell, vision inspection, AMR fleet and tote station, which can easily cost millions.
  • Teach by demonstration: remote-control the robot, record camera and joint data, train on GPUs, deploy. Tasks are scheduled with no-code drag and drop.
  • Hot-swap batteries keep it running three shifts.
  • Hardware and AI models will commoditise. The lasting advantage is the application and your own data, so start small to get the data flywheel going.

The first question isn't which robot to buy. It's which repetitive task we'd record first.

09 · 3:15

The Smart Expressway: AI on Malaysia's highways

  • Ts. Muhammad Hadzrin Mahdi · Head Customer Solutions, TERAS Teknologi

Highway tolls are moving to barrier-free, drive-through payment.

MLFFMulti-Lane Free Flow, the end goal
1,000+ kmof PLUS highway in scope
  • Detection today: RFID, number-plate recognition and video analytics classify vehicles and prevent double charging.
  • The path: Single Lane Free Flow first, then Multi-Lane Free Flow, where you drive through at highway speed and the toll is charged to a registered e-wallet.
  • No on-board unit: unlike Singapore's GNSS-based ERP, the approach relies on cameras and RFID. Detection rules are standardised across every concessionaire.
  • AI for safety: highway CCTV detects people at risk on a stretch of road and dispatches patrol cars. This is done with the state government.

In regulated industries, standardising the detection rules matters as much as the AI model.

10 · 3:55

Make AI Real: how AI moves from digital to physical

  • Tindaro Danze · CEO, Siemens Malaysia

AI is a brain in a jar until you connect it to the shop floor.

3,000smart factories targeted by 2030 (NIMP 2030)
<5%of that target reached so far
80%of industrial data never used
  • Consumer AI can be 80% right. The shop floor needs close to 100%, especially when robots act on its output.
  • 1 · Connect: join the silos (PLM, ERP, MES, machines) with a shared data model so AI has context.
  • 2 · Test safely: Siemens simulates humanoids in a digital twin (NVIDIA Omniverse) first. “The virtual fails before the physical.” Cobots in its own packaging line show how hard edge cases are: grip, angle, and whether the box will close.
  • 3 · Scale where it pays. Don't pilot things that don't meet a business need.
  • Ready today: an engineering agent for TIA Portal. You describe the intent and it generates PLC function blocks. It's safer because it stays digital, and it helps with an engineer shortage: semiconductors need ~60,000 engineers.

Late adopters can leapfrog, the way developing countries skipped landlines for mobile. Start with connecting data, not with robots.

11 · 4:20

Crossing the AI Chasm: from fear to adoption in finance GBS

  • Oh Ming Poh · General Manager, Wilmar Global Business Services

One hour a week of AI time led to 1,300 automations in three months.

150k → ~500kinvoices a year, without adding headcount
1,300staff-built automations in 3 months
~600 hsaved per week across ~300 people
  • The question was never whether to use AI. It was how to scale without adding people, across 77 entities in 5 countries.
  • The journey: transactional work (2013) → standardisation → RPA → AI.
  • Playbook: find a real pain point → simplify first (stop reports nobody reads) → build something close to the person's daily work → celebrate small wins.
  • One hour a week of AI exploration for the whole team: 260 use cases after two months and 1,300 after three.
  • Copying spreads it: one finance staff member's no-code Power Automate flow was reused by colleagues, saving about 600 hours a week.
  • Use cases: accounts-payable three-way match, vendor reconciliation, billing reminders, reading bank remittances, and real-time reporting.
  • Guardrails: a solid data foundation, a safe sandbox, data controls, and leaders who walk the talk.

Trust comes from people seeing it, doing it, then embracing it. Announcements don't close the gap.
